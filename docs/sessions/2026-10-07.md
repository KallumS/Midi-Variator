## What the tests found

**The walking bass was "the tune".** Develop took the highest note of each
moment as the tune; between the chords of the walking-bass fixture the
only note struck is the bass, so inverting "the tune" would have inverted
half the bass line. A note under something held over is no longer the
tune. Found by a test written to catch the tune falling into the chords.

**The tune could sink into a held chord.** It was kept above the notes
struck with it, not above a chord held from beat 1. Missed by the first
test (the piano fixture's tune is far above its chords); a close tune over
add9 chords now catches it.

**A sequence could add a semitone.** Moving by scale steps is not moving
by a fixed interval: D under E, moved up a step, is E under F. Only notes
struck together were checked; the tune over a held chord was not. Found
by the same close-tune test.

**Adding the arpeggio move changed every other piece.** One more move in
Chord quality's list shifted the dice for music with no arpeggios; a 1.2
seed for popChords changed two of four chords and failed the likeness
test. The move is now offered only where there are arpeggios.

**The arpeggio reading was too strict, then too loose.** "No steps at all"
missed a G7 Alberti bass (B G F G); allowing steps let the riff (E G E B A)
in. The rules that held: a pitch class comes round again, leaps at least
half the steps, at most one pair of pitch classes a step apart. And two
chords in a bar were read as one four-note chord until halves came first
when they differ.

**An Alberti C could not become Cmaj7** - nothing doubled to give way.
Every other strike of the commonest note takes the new tone (C G E G ->
C G E B).

**An echo was not its original.** The first statement was made with the
series memory steering it, the echo without; they drifted. An echo now
gets a copy of the memory as its original found it.

**A filled chord note went to the bass's track** when tune, chords and
bass were three items: the bass was nearest. Nearest but the bass.

**Develop was slow.** Four variations of a 2,000-note piece at 100% took
2-3.6s (1.2: 0.15s): every moved note asked every note in the piece. An
index by time made it 0.1-0.4s; 1,680 variations were compared note for
note before and after - identical.
